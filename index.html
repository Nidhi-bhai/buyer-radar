How a channel gets its score

Each channel is scored from 1 to 5 on Money (is there a budget), Authority (can this person or company say yes), and Need (does the channel actually deal in this product). The total is the sum, out of 15. Ties break by Money, then Authority, then Need, then channel type.

Scores on companies, shows, communities, marketplaces, and job titles are editorial. The reason says why, and the source is a public page: a 2022 NAICS title from the U.S. Census Bureau, an O*NET occupation, or the channel's own site. They are not measurements, and they are not buyer counts.

Importing countries are different. Money follows the rank of that reporter's primaryValue in the UN Comtrade public preview for one HS code, one year, imports from the world: 1st scores 5, 2nd–3rd score 4, 4th–10th score 3. The dollar amount on the card is the API's literal value, not a BuyerRadar estimate. If the HS heading is broader than the product, Need is marked down and the page says so.

The free result is the top 5 channels. The $19 report is the full ranking plus an outreach angle for each channel, as Markdown and as a PDF. PayPal Smart Buttons read PAYPAL_CLIENT_ID from config.js. Until that placeholder is replaced, the button is not loaded. No PayPal secret belongs in this repository. A static site cannot verify a payment on a server.

Services such as software have no merchandise HS code. Those reports say that, and they do not invent trade numbers. The Comtrade snapshot is refreshed with python3 scripts/refresh-trade.py because the public API does not send CORS headers a browser can use.
